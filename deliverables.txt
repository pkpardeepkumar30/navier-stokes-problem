Principal result and limitation:
At the illustrative 1 mm / 1 m/s anchor, the inverse-law limit gives radii of approximately 0.334 nm at 0.01c and 0.0334 nm at 0.1c. These are formal extrapolations. Mach/Knudsen screens arise much earlier under the stated reference assumptions; a full dimensional profile/viscosity match and actual gradient field remain uncomputed. The detailed paper imposes a necessary h<exp(-10) ceiling, so wider introductory exponent sweeps are labelled illustrative, and h=0 is used only as a limit.

Working title:
"How small does a collapsing vortex become—and which fluid assumption fails first?"

Outputs:
- A concise source/notation note identifying the paper version, radius and speed definitions, time scaling, admissible exponent choices, and relevant source equations.
- One reproducible notebook with reusable scaling/calibration functions.
- One short executable article explaining the derivation and physical interpretation.
- A log-log plot of characteristic velocity versus core radius, with clearly labelled physical reference bands and formal-extrapolation regions.
- A table of radii at selected speeds, including sonic/hypersonic markers for a specified fluid state and the formal extrapolations 0.01c and 0.1c. Include c only if it adds explanatory value and label it unattainable for massive fluid matter.
- A sensitivity figure/table showing the effect of reference radius, reference speed, exponent, and any unresolved profile factors.
- A calibration ledger recording the actual assumptions and which physical markers have been evaluated. Start with a general dimensionless curve and a small number of illustrative cases; use air and water reference properties where supported, without calling either a fully calibrated solution prematurely.
- Exported figure/table data and reproduction instructions.

Completion criteria:
- The radius-speed exponent is derived from cited source scalings, with dimensional consistency checked.
- Reference values correspond to a nonzero-speed stage of the collapse; speed alone is not presented as determining an absolute radius.
- The dimensional mapping accounts for viscosity and profile normalization, or the missing calibration is explicitly identified.
- The interpretation distinguishes Mach-number screening, gas Knudsen criteria, and liquid molecular/relaxation considerations. Temperature and pressure conditions accompany property values.
- Any proxy for a local gradient length is explicit. No single universal atomic/molecular cutoff is claimed.
- Results separate the formal classical extrapolation from the range plausibly described by a real fluid model.
- Numerical checks cover the inverse relation, units, and limiting power-law behavior; plotted values can be regenerated from the exported parameters.

Scope boundary:
No Navier-Stokes time integration, full profile reconstruction, all-order corrections, or molecular simulation is required. Mention possible smaller correction scales as a limitation rather than claiming they have already been quantified.
